Colour is personal, and these notes explain why some pairings tend to work.

## Why does green work with cream, beige and mustard?

Green brings the outdoors in and feels calm and grounded. Cream and beige soften it and keep the room light. Mustard adds warmth without fighting the green. A deep green with cream feels classic, while a softer green with beige feels relaxed. Use mustard in small amounts, such as a cushion or a throw. The mix works because the neutral carries the colour and the mustard adds a friendly lift.

## Why does grey work with mustard, teal and blush?

Grey is a quiet base that lets other colours speak. Mustard adds warmth to cool grey. Teal adds depth and feels calm next to grey. Blush softens the whole scheme and keeps it from looking cold. Choose one accent to lead and let the others support it. This pairing works well in rooms with plenty of natural light. Add texture through cushions or a rug to keep it from feeling flat.

## Why does navy work with camel, cream and brass?

Navy is deep and grounding. Camel and cream lighten it and keep the room from feeling heavy. Brass adds a warm metallic touch that suits navy's depth. Use brass in small pieces such as a lamp base or a table edge. This mix feels smart without being formal. It suits a living room that doubles as a place to work or read.

## Why does maroon work with cream and gold?

Maroon is rich and warm. Cream gives it space to breathe and keeps the room bright. Gold adds a little glow that echoes the warmth of maroon. Keep gold to details rather than large surfaces. This pairing suits a dining or sitting room where you want a sense of occasion. A cream wall and a maroon sofa do most of the work on their own.

## Do neutrals go with everything?

Yes, neutrals are the easy base for most schemes. Cream, beige, grey and sand sit quietly beside stronger colours. They also work together in layers of light and dark. A neutral sofa gives you room to change cushions and rugs later. Add one or two colours you love, and let the neutral hold them together. Soft furnishings are the cheapest way to try a colour before you commit to a large piece.

## What do tone and warmth mean?

Tone is how light or dark a colour is. Warmth is whether a colour leans towards yellow and red or towards blue and grey. A room with cool light often welcomes warm colours, and a sunny room can carry cooler ones. Keep tone and warmth in mind when you compare fabrics side by side.

## How do I choose a colour in natural light?

Take fabric samples home before you decide. Look at them in the morning, in the afternoon and at night with the lamps on. Hold each sample against the wall and next to the floor. Natural light changes how a colour reads through the day. A shade that looks right in the shop may look different at home. Choose the one you still like in every light.
