The guidance below is general. Pieces are made to order, so the team confirms sizes and options at your visit.

## How do I measure my room for a sofa?

Measure the length of each wall where the sofa could go. Measure doorways, lifts and stairs on the route in. Leave space to walk past the sofa; around 60 to 90 cm is a typical guide. Mark the footprint on the floor with tape so you can see how it feels. Check power points and curtains that the sofa might block. Bring the measurements and a simple sketch to your visit.

## Should I choose an L-shape, a U-shape or a 3+2+1?

An L-shape fits a corner and gives a chaise or open end for lounging. A U-shape wraps around a coffee table and seats a group, but it needs a large room. A 3+2+1 set gives flexible seating that you can rearrange. An L-shape suits open-plan living. A U-shape feels generous but can crowd a narrow room. A 3+2+1 works when you want separate seats and a clear walkway. Measure first, then choose the shape that leaves room to move.

## How deep should a sofa seat be?

A typical seat depth is around 55 to 65 cm. A shallower seat gives more upright support for sitting and talking. A deeper seat is better for lounging and for taller people. Very deep seats can be hard for shorter people to get out of. Sit on a few pieces at the showroom to feel the difference. The team can confirm the seat depth of each piece.

## What suits a home with kids and pets?

Choose a tight weave and a mid or dark colour if the sofa gets heavy use. Patterns hide small marks better than pale plain fabric. Avoid very light colours in a busy room unless you are happy to clean often. Ask about removable covers, which are easier to wash. A throw on the seat adds protection and is simple to replace. Trim pet claws and keep food and drinks away from the sofa.

## What works in a small flat?

Measure the lift, corridor and door before you choose. A slim-arm sofa or a small L-shape can make a compact room feel less crowded. Light colours and legs that show floor space help a room feel larger. Avoid a U-shape unless the room is generous. Keep a clear walkway. A 3+2+1 set can be split between rooms if space is tight. Bring your floor plan to the visit.
